Skeletal System

The framework of bones that supports the body, protects organs, and enables movement.

Overview

The skeletal system consists of 206 bones in an adult, connected by joints, ligaments, and cartilage. It provides structural support, protects vital organs, stores minerals like calcium and phosphorus, and produces blood cells in the bone marrow.

Main Functions

Support: Gives the body its shape and posture. Protection: Skull protects the brain, ribs protect heart and lungs. Movement: Bones act as levers for muscles. Storage: Stores calcium, phosphorus, and fat. Blood cell production: Bone marrow creates red and white blood cells.

Key Parts

Skull (22 bones), vertebral column (33 vertebrae), rib cage (24 ribs), sternum, pelvis, and limbs. Joints include hinge (knee), ball-and-socket (hip), pivot (neck), and gliding (wrist).

Common Issues

Osteoporosis, fractures, arthritis, scoliosis, and rickets are common. Adequate calcium, vitamin D, and weight-bearing exercise help maintain bone health.
