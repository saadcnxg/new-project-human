Immune System

The body's defense network against bacteria, viruses, and other threats.

Overview

The immune system identifies and neutralizes pathogens, infected cells, and abnormal cells. It includes innate (fast, general) and adaptive (slow, specific) immunity.

Main Functions

Barriers: Skin, mucus, and stomach acid block pathogens. Innate immunity: Macrophages and neutrophils attack broadly. Adaptive immunity: T and B cells target specific threats and remember them. Memory: Enables long-term protection and vaccines.

Key Parts

White blood cells (T cells, B cells, macrophages), lymph nodes, spleen, thymus, tonsils, and bone marrow.

Common Issues

Allergies, autoimmune diseases, immunodeficiency, and severe infections. Sleep, diet, exercise, and vaccines strengthen immunity.
