Thymus

A training school for T-cells, at its largest in childhood and shrinking with age.

Overview

The thymus is a bilobed lymphatic organ in the upper chest. It matures T lymphocytes, which are essential for adaptive immunity. It is largest in childhood and gradually shrinks after puberty.

Main Functions

T-cell maturation: Produces and selects functional T lymphocytes. Immune tolerance: Removes self-reactive cells. Hormone production: Secretes thymosin and other factors.

Key Parts

Cortex, medulla, and Hassall's corpuscles.

Common Issues

Thymoma, thymic hyperplasia, and myasthenia gravis. Healthy lifestyle supports thymic function.
